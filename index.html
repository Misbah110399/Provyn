Pulls in the loan master record.

Line 20 · who calls this

Called by LNPAY01, the nightly payment run.

LNPAY01 calls it through a variable, WS-CALC-PGM, not a literal name. Simple text search misses that. Provyn traces the value back to resolve the call.

22 ACCEPT WS-TODAY FROM DATE

Line 22

Reads today’s date, with a two-digit year.

COBOL’s DATE gives YYMMDD, so the century has to be guessed. The next block does exactly that.

Source LNCALC1.cbl:22
23 IF WS-TODAY-YY < 50
24 MOVE 20 TO WS-CENTURY
25 ELSE
26 MOVE 19 TO WS-CENTURY
27 END-IF

Lines 23–27 · business rule · timing

Years 00–49 are read as 20xx, 50–99 as 19xx.

A century window left over from Y2K. It works today, but from 2050 this program will think it is 1950. That goes straight into the risk register.

Risk · fails in 2050Rule · LNCALC1.cbl:23
28 COMPUTE WS-MONTHLY-RATE = LM-NOTE-RATE / 1200

Line 28

Turns the yearly rate into a monthly one.

The note rate is stored as a percentage, so dividing by 1,200 gives a monthly decimal. A 6.000% loan becomes 0.005 a month.

Source LNCALC1.cbl:28
29 COMPUTE LK-INTEREST ROUNDED =
LM-PRIN-BAL * WS-MONTHLY-RATE

Line 29

Interest due = balance × monthly rate.

Rounded to the cent. On a $200,000 balance at 6%, that is $1,000.00 for the month. The new system must round the same way, and the tests check it.

Source LNCALC1.cbl:29Test case generated
30 IF LK-INTEREST > LM-PI-PMT
31 MOVE LM-PI-PMT TO LK-INTEREST
32 END-IF

Lines 30–32 · business rule · money

Interest is never more than the scheduled payment.

If the interest would be bigger than the P&I payment, it is capped. What happens to the difference? The code doesn’t say, so Provyn asks the business owner to confirm before anything is converted.

Owner must confirmRule · LNCALC1.cbl:30
33 GOBACK.

Line 33

Hands the interest back to LNPAY01.

LNPAY01 then posts the payment to the loan master. Together the two form the recommended pilot: contained, batch, and easy to prove record by record.

Questions

What risk and IT teams ask first

Does our code leave our environment?

No. Provyn runs in your Kubernetes cluster, and the only AI it calls is the Azure OpenAI deployment in your own tenant. We can work inside your environment under your access controls.

What if the AI gets something wrong?

Every statement cites the file and line it came from, so a reviewer can check it in seconds. Nothing is final until a named expert signs it off, and converted code is proven by running old and new on the same data.

Do you need access to our mainframe?

No. For the assessment we need an export of the source: programs, copybooks, JCL, CICS definitions and DB2 DDL. Nothing is executed.

What do we get at the end of the assessment?

A modernization plan as a PDF and an interactive page: a decision for every program, a phased roadmap, the dependency map, the business rules catalog, a risk register and the evidence ledger behind it all.

How is it priced?

The assessment is a fixed price agreed upfront, based on the size of the estate. Pilots and programs are scoped from the plan, so you always know what the next step costs before you commit.
